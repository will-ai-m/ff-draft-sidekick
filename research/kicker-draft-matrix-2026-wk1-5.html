What actually makes a fantasy kicker — the factors behind the chips

  1. Implied team total (Vegas). The single most information-dense pregame number for scoring volume — more projected points means more FG + XP tries. Chip: green ≥ 24 implied points, red < 21, yellow between. A spread of ≥ 7 either way adds a script chip: big favorites control games and kick; big underdogs chase touchdowns and go for it.
  2. Venue — dome vs. outdoors. Indoor games score ~9% more (46.2 vs 42.4 pts/gm) and wind is the #1 physics factor for kicks: distance and accuracy degrade past ~15 mph, crosswinds worst. Chip: green for domes/closed roofs (ATL, ARI, DAL, DET, HOU, IND, LAC, LAR, LV, MIN, NO), red for chronic wind bowls (Highmark, Soldier, Cleveland lakefront, Gillette, MetLife), gray for ordinary outdoor sites. Denver is green: altitude adds ~5 yards of range. Weeks 1–5 are September, so cold/snow isn't yet in play — wind venues are the only real weather risk.
  3. Opposing defense. Elite defenses shrink scoring drives and kick attempts. Chip: red vs a top-10 projected defense, green vs 22–32. (Caveat baked into blurbs: a bend-don't-break top defense can still force field goals.)
  4. The kicker's own leg + job security (row-level, in the notes): 50+ yard range turns 3s into 5s in most scoring formats, and camp battles/injuries are the biggest bust risk at the position.
  5. Own-offense shape (row-level): you want an offense that moves the ball but stalls — red-zone trip rate is sticky year to year, red-zone conversion is noise. Elite TD offenses pay in XPs; FG-heavy offenses (Pittsburgh-style) pay in 3s. Aggressive 4th-down coaches steal attempts.

Draft-day takeaways

  • Aubrey (K1, DAL) is the rare kicker worth a round: best leg alive, #4 offense, dome home, and a Wk 2 WAS shootout (implied 28). Blemishes: a windy MetLife opener, and Wk 3 vs BAL moving to São Paulo, outdoors.
  • Bates (K2, DET) has the best early slate on the board (K-SOS +2.6) — three bottom-11 defenses (NYJ, CAR, ARI) and an implied total of 24.8+ every single week.
  • Loop (K16, BAL) is the late-round sleeper the chips flag: second-best K-SOS (+2.4) and a 26.5+ implied total in all five weeks — nobody else clears 26 more than twice.
  • Dicker (K3, LAC) opens with back-to-back indoor smashes vs ARI (implied 28.5) and LV (25.5), then cools off at BUF/SEA.
  • Fairbairn (K6, HOU) is the value pick: four of five opponents have bottom-11 defenses and NRG's roof erases weather.
  • Little (K5, JAX) is the talent-vs-schedule tradeoff — the biggest leg in football, but DEN/NE/PHI defenses in weeks 2–5.
  • Fade early: Ryland (ARI) has the slate's worst K-SOS (−2.0) despite the dome, and Folk (NYJ), Szmyt (CLE), Slye (TEN) pair barren offenses with wind venues and low totals. Butker (KC) and Fitzgerald (CAR) hit bye in week 5 — though Butker's four pre-bye weeks grade out well (+1.8).

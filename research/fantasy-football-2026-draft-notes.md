# 3. Player-by-player, in FantasyPros order

## Tier 1 — FP 1–10

### 1. Jahmyr Gibbs (RB, DET)
**Consensus RB1. Unanimous.**
- **J&H:** Both have him #1. 25.1 half-PPR ppg in the 8 games David Montgomery missed; Montgomery is gone and Pacheco "is no David Montgomery"; 20 ppg in 25 career games at 60%+ snaps and he should push past 70%; Lions favored in 14 games (2nd most); new OC Drew Petzing ranks 4th in generating 10+ yard runs; a bad defense means shootouts, and the schedule is dome-heavy. The only knock was the contract holdout — now resolved.
- **FFN:** 27.4 ppg in 6 career games without Montgomery (25+ in five straight) on 24.3 touches. **#1 in RB fantasy points over expectation (+5.0).** Highest odds of any player to score 10+ TDs. Lions have the easiest schedule in the NFL, 11 of the final 13 fantasy-season games in a dome, and an offense concentrated into four players. ❌ One flag: Detroit was 5th in explosive runs to Penei Sewell's side and 22nd running the other way.
- **FFN (8/20):** 5th in scrimmage yards (1,839) and a member of the 1.4+ YPRR / 22%+ target-share-per-route-run club. Also just extended, alongside Bijan and Jonathan Taylor.

### 2. Bijan Robinson (RB, ATL)
- **J&H:** Both #2. RB3 on RB8 usage; 20 ppg when he reaches 15 touches; 79% snap share; a fantasy-playoff run of 175, 168 and 229 total yards. The path to overall #1 is red zone: **only 23 career carries inside the 5 across three years** — Derrick Henry had 24 last season alone. ❌ Atlanta is favored in **two games**, and the current QB1 is "a complete nobody" with Penix injured.
- **FFN:** #3 in points over expectation (+3.9), #2 in expected ppg (18.1). Now the 2nd-most guaranteed money ever for a RB. One of only four first-round rookie RBs ever with 7.0+ rushing and 7.0+ receiving ppg.
- **FFN (8/20):** **Led the NFL in scrimmage yards (2,298)** and is in the 1.4+ YPRR / 22%+ target-share-per-route-run club (with CMC, Gibbs, Dylan Sampson, Kenny Gainwell) — the receiving-usage tier that separates the true three-down backs.

### 3. Ja'Marr Chase (WR, CIN)
- **J&H:** Both #3. 21.5 half-PPR ppg in healthy-Burrow games over three years; WR1 in expected points; top-5 in ppg in 4 of 5 seasons; more 30+ point weeks than any WR. Only mild flag is Cincinnati's under-center experiment.
- **FFN:** Bengals have the 2nd-easiest schedule and are favored in 15 of 17. 2nd in routes run (613). 4th in YPRR vs two-high (2.49). **Holds the single-season record for "highly accurate" deep targets (20 in 2021).** 4th in ppg excluding injury-report weeks (19.3). Member of the elite press-coverage / sub-30%-slot club.
- **FFN (8/20):** 4th in the NFL in 3rd/4th-down receiving yards (423).

### 4. Puka Nacua (WR, LAR) 🚨
- **J&H:** Josh 4 / **Hayden 2**. 23.8 PPR ppg; the most receiving yards per game in NFL history (95); no WR3 on the roster so they run 13 personnel with him as the lone receiver; 8 receptions per game on a 33% target share outside the red zone. The ceiling is gated by the red zone — Davante Adams out-targeted him 31–10 there and 28–4 in the end zone. Hayden thinks a 400-point season is live. Contract year.
- **FFN:** **#1 in ppg excluding injury weeks (24.6). #1 in YPRR vs two-high (3.40). The only WR with three separate 3.00+ YPRR seasons against both man and zone.** 109.9 receiving yards per game over the final 11 weeks. 4th-best rookie YPRR since 2015.
- **MH:** 🚨 The cloud got darker on 8/14 — per Schefter he "could face suspension" after **biting a woman** and making an "unprovoked antisemitic sentiment," and he was separately cut by a glass window pane at Michael Rubin's white party. MH dropped him a spot and now says: "At this point, I'd rather take Amon-Ra St. Brown."
- **FFN (8/20):** **5th in the NFL in 3rd/4th-down receiving yards (399)** — one more line item on a profile where the only real question is availability.
- **J&H (8/18–19 update):** 🚨 Now also nursing **groin/psoas soreness** and missing camp (McVay: "we're going to err on the side of caution"). He was NOT in the batch of suspensions the NFL handed down 8/17, but the case is still pending. Hayden briefly moved him behind Chase, then restored him at **#2 overall in full PPR** — "by the time most of you are drafting, Puka will be my second overall player"; 24.0 full-PPR ppg last year was 3.4 clear of the WR2, and the Davante trade means the red-zone role could finally be his ("we're talking an all-time season").

### 5. Jaxon Smith-Njigba (WR, SEA)
**Both hosts fade him 3 spots.**
- **J&H:** Both 8. 99.6 receiving yards per game including the postseason; a 35% target share; only 8% of targets were screens, so he earned it downfield; 3.73 receiving yards per team pass attempt; led the NFL in yards and TDs off play action. ❌ Regression is coming — a 35% share, 10.5 yards/target and +10% CPOE are all unsustainable; he lost OC Klint Kubiak; he's done it once.
- **FFN:** #2 in ppg excluding injury weeks (21.8), #2 in YPRR vs two-high (2.60), 97.4 receiving yards per game over the final 11 weeks, and 16 highly-accurate deep targets (T-4th all-time). ❌ Seattle scored the **fewest** points in the final two minutes of the 4th quarter (6) despite going 14–3.
- **MH:** Playcaller-change LOSER — new OC Brian Fleury is a first-time play caller (a former 49ers run-game coordinator/TE coach), and "I'd still bet on regression from this offense after losing their top-notch playcaller to a HC gig."

### 6. Amon-Ra St. Brown (WR, DET)
- **J&H:** **Josh 5** / Hayden 7. 100+ catches four straight years; three straight seasons with double-digit TDs and 115+ receptions; **led the NFL with 34 red-zone targets (66 over two years)**; 12 of 16 fantasy-season games indoors; 21 PPR ppg in the 10-game sample without Montgomery. "As safe as it gets."
- **FFN:** #3 in ppg excluding injury weeks (19.6). 3rd in combined red-zone + downfield targets per game (4.1). Led the league in receptions where he was tackled inside the 5 (eight). One of the four concentrated Lions.
- **MH (8/14):** **"The S&P 500 of fantasy assets"** — WR3 three years in a row, top-10 four years in a row, one of the league's softest schedules. Now their preferred pick over Puka.

### 7. Christian McCaffrey (RB, SF)
**The one player all three sources flag.**
- **J&H:** Hayden 5 / Josh 6. RB1 overall in both expected AND actual points; 47% of team touches, the most in the NFL. ❌ 450 touches — Arian Foster, DeMarco Murray and Saquon all cratered the following year at ages 26–27, and he's 30; 37th in 10+ yard run rate, 36th in yards after contact; Shanahan wants to scale him back.
- **FFN:** #1 in expected ppg (23.6); #2 in expected fantasy points per route run (0.48); 26.6 opportunities per game.
- **MH:** **Listed as an RB BUST.** 448 touches in 2025 including the playoffs; he had 403 in 2019 and 417 in 2023, then played 3 and 4 games respectively. "More reasonable than ever to question whether he's capable of leading your team to a championship."
- **FFN (8/20):** The volume case restated three ways — **#1 among RBs in expected TDs (22.5 rushing + receiving)**, a **45.0% TD rate on carries inside the 5** (min. 10), **236.4 PPR points from receiving alone**, and 2nd in scrimmage yards (2,126). He's also in the exclusive 2025 club of RBs at 1.4+ yards per route run AND a 22%+ target share per route run (with Bijan, Gibbs, Dylan Sampson and Kenny Gainwell).
- **J&H (8/17–19 update):** 🚨 Missed a full week of practice with unspecified "tightness" — echoes of the vague 2024 reporting. **Hayden moved Jonathan Taylor ahead of him (JT 5th, CMC 6th)** but won't drop him further: "how many more players can I move him down before you're sacrificing league-winning ability?" Hayden's theory: the soreness may be contract-related (Bijan and Gibbs now make ~2x CMC), in which case he moves right back up. Shanahan has since denied the contract angle. # 5. Cross-cutting themes

### 1. The strategic core of J&H's board
A systematic **fade of round-2 pass-catchers** (A.J. Brown, Drake London, Trey McBride, Chris Olave) in order to buy **round-3/4 running backs with goal-line jobs** (Kenneth Walker, Breece Hall, Travis Etienne, Josh Jacobs, Javonte Williams) — plus one genuine industry-wide contrarian call on Ashton Jeanty.

### 2. Heavy personnel is the dominant scheme theme
Every source is chasing the same insight: fewer receivers on the field means each remaining receiver earns targets more easily. JSN's breakout is the reference case (**51% of his production came from 2-or-fewer-WR sets on only 38% of his routes**). Applied to: Ladd McConkey (only 10% of routes in those sets last year), Brock Bowers, Colston Loveland, Kyle Pitts, Parker Washington.

### 3. Red-zone regression is the most-cited single lever
Nico Collins, Zay Flowers, Bijan Robinson, Puka Nacua, CeeDee Lamb, Saquon Barkley, Breece Hall and Justin Jefferson all have documented cases resting substantially on touchdown regression.

### 4. Play-caller changes as the market inefficiency
MH devoted its 8/7 issue to this; the same exercise last year flagged the Bears, Jaguars and Patriots as ascending offenses.
- **Mike McDaniel → LAC** ✅ the most-hyped move: Herbert (MH's "this year's Drake Maye"), Hampton, McConkey, Johnston, Keaton Mitchell
- **Klint Kubiak (HC) → LV** ✅ Bowers, and Jeanty as a "McShanahan" wide-zone winner
- **Drew Petzing → DET** ✅ Gibbs and LaPorta (Petzing led the NFL in TE target share three straight years) — but a ❌ for Jameson Williams
- **Declan Doyle → BAL** ✅ heavy play action: Lamar (QB1 in play-action passer rating twice), Zay Flowers (#1 in play-action YPRR), and Derrick Henry most of all (Doyle's influences rank 2nd and 13th in backfield expected ppg)
- **Eric Bieniemy → KC** ✅ his 2018–22 offenses ranked 1st/6th/1st/3rd/1st in yards: TD upside for Kenneth Walker, a "possible resurgence" for Kelce
- **Kevin Stefanski + Tommy Rees → ATL** ✅ Kyle Pitts (167 TE targets per year in Stefanski offenses) and Drake London
- **Ben Johnson (yr 2) → CHI** ✅ Swift, Loveland, Burden, Odunze
- **Todd Monken → CLE** ❌ for Judkins — Monken always splits rushing and receiving between two backs (enter Dylan Sampson)
- **Matt Nagy → NYG** ❌ KC declined every year of his OC tenure; his Bears never finished better than 21st in yards
- **Frank Reich → NYJ** ❌ the harshest verdict anywhere: "Avoid Jets at all cost"
- **Brian Fleury → SEA** ❌ a first-time play caller replacing Kubiak — MH bets on regression for Darnold and JSN
- Positive early camp reviews for the other new OCs: Sean Mannion (PHI), Zac Robinson (TB), David Blough (WAS)

FFN identified four WRs costing meaningfully more in high-stakes formats than in home leagues — **Christian Watson, Parker Washington, Quentin Johnston, Jayden Higgins** — all four of whom J&H also rank above their ESPN ADP. MH's 8/14 WR Preview then independently listed **three of the four** (Watson and Washington as breakouts, Higgins as a sleeper) — the tightest cross-source agreement in the whole dataset.

### 9. FFN's reach math — the first framework anyone offers for *when* to go off-board (8/20)

Every source in this document argues players are mispriced. FFN's 8/20 issue is the first to ask how often being early is actually right, and it's the most portable idea in the whole dataset because it applies to every other call here.

**The study:** across the last six seasons, **9,357 head-to-head same-position matchups inside the top 120 picks.** Among players drafted **12–20 picks apart at the same position, the earlier player outscored the later one only 61% of the time.** Compounding it: **reach twice in a row and you're right on both roughly 15% of the time.**

The practical read is that a one-round positional reach buys you a 61/39 edge, not a certainty — so the reach has to be paid for by a mechanism, not a vibe. FFN then names exactly four players they think clear that bar:

| Reach | Pos | The stated mechanism |
|---|---|---|
| **Colston Loveland** | TE | Projected 6.9 targets/game on a **21% target share** — and TEs above a 20% team target share break out **59.1%** of the time vs **13.5%** otherwise |
| **Jaylen Waddle** | WR | Projected **26% target share** in Denver vs a **24.3% median** for a middle-round WR |
| **Jaylen Warren** | RB | The stated archetype: "running backs in split backfields with high target shares" |
| **Trevor Lawrence** | QB | Projected rushing-volume increase; a career-best scoring offense "already discounted for regression" |

**How this scores the rest of the document.** Three of the four are already J&H above-market calls (Loveland, Waddle, and Hayden's round-7 Jaylen Warren), and Lawrence is an MH breakout — so the reach list is really a fourth vote on existing consensus buys rather than four new names. The more useful application is inverted: the 61% figure is the strongest available argument *against* the boldest single calls in here — Hayden's Kenneth Walker at 9th overall (~14 spots early), Josh's Saquon at 7th, Hayden's Rashee Rice fade to 44th. Those are multi-round deviations, and this study says the base rate for being right on a single one-round deviation is already only marginally better than a coin flip.

Related repeat-rate baselines from the same issue, useful for the same purpose — since 2016, elite fantasy performers repeat the following season at **QB 37% / RB 43% / WR 46% / TE 50%** (top-6 PPR TEs specifically repeat 50%). And one structural rule: since 2003, **every top-5 PPR running back had either 90+ targets or 10+ TDs** — a clean filter to run against the RB-heavy builds J&H recommend.

# 6. Appendix: methodology

### Tooling
- **Transcripts:** [`yt-dlp`](https://github.com/yt-dlp/yt-dlp) (open source) to pull YouTube auto-caption tracks — no API key required:
  ```bash
  yt-dlp --write-auto-subs --skip-download --sub-langs "en.*" --sub-format vtt <video-url>
  ```
  VTT then stripped of timing cues and rolling-window duplication, and re-wrapped for reading. 15 videos → ~204,000 words (the four post-8/12 videos added ~47,000).
- **Rankings:** FantasyPros embeds its consensus data as a JSON blob (`ecrData`) in the cheatsheets page HTML; parsed directly for rank, position rank, team and expert spread across all 819 players.
- **Articles:** fetched and converted to text; Morning Huddle's beehiiv paywall truncates three of the four posts after their first section. FFN's 8/20 issue was fetched the same way, then re-queried with targeted factual questions to resolve ambiguous chart labels rather than relying on a single summarization pass.

### Known limitations
1. **MH paywall — resolved.** Three of the original four MH posts were truncated mid-article; the complete text was later supplied and merged in. The 8/14 WR Preview, 8/17 QB Preview and 8/19 TE Preview were added the same way. All seven MH issues are fully represented.
2. **Auto-caption name errors.** The transcripts consistently mangle proper nouns. Names were resolved from surrounding context and cross-checked against the FantasyPros roster. A handful of low-confidence single mentions were dropped rather than guessed at.
3. **J&H rankings span multiple recording dates** (now 7/27–8/19). Several moved during the window — most notably Kenneth Walker (12 → 9), Jeremiyah Love (25 → RB16 → nearly 15th overall → 26th post-injury), Stefon Diggs (WR70s → WR40) and Jaylen Waddle (down post-scare, back up post-return). Both values are noted where they differ; entries tagged "J&H (8/13–8/19 update)" supersede earlier stated ranks.
5. **Half-PPR vs full-PPR.** The board order and section 4's main table are half-PPR; **Hayden's 8/19 Top 100 is FULL PPR** and deliberately shifts QBs down (~a round) and reception-heavy players up. Don't cross-compare the two without that adjustment.
6. **Auto-caption mangling was severe in the 8/13–8/19 videos.** Names resolved from context include: "Bill Crocky Merit"/"Jacquory Crouch"/"J.D. McKissic"/"Jerrick McKinnon" → Jacory Croskey-Merritt; "Jamaal Williams (bellcow)"/"Jahmyrri Gibbs Price" → Jeremiyah Love / Jadarian Price contexts; "Cam Akers" (Giants) → Cam Skattebo; "Jaleel Johnson" → Jonah Coleman; "Kalen Ballage" → Kyle Monangai; "Asim Zant"/"Achane JT" → Ashton Jeanty; "Ichiga"/"Ibuka" → Egbuka; "Jaylen Null" → Jaylin Noel (Texans slot WR); "Elijah Serat" → Elijah Sarrat (Ravens WR).
